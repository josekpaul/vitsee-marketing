About VitSee

Healthcare pricing shouldn't be a secret.

We built VitSee to turn massive, fragmented healthcare pricing data into insights a real person can use before the bill arrives.

TBs of pricing data processed
1000s of ACA plans
100,000s of providers
Millions of negotiated rates

The problem

Under federal transparency rules, insurers and hospitals now publish their negotiated rates. But they publish them as terabytes of sprawling, inconsistent machine-readable files that no consumer could ever actually read. So people still choose care blind, often paying thousands more than they needed to for the same procedure a few miles away.

What we do

VitSee ingests ACA Public Use Files, CMS Transparency in Coverage machine-readable files, and CMS quality data, then turns that firehose into something simple: a clear comparison of what a procedure will actually cost you, at which providers, ranked by both price and quality.

Who it's for

Anyone that needs healthcare services and wants to shop for the best value. VitSee exists so you never have to guess whether "cheap" also means "good."

Why now

Healthcare costs keep rising, and government subsidies are covering less of the gap, so the share of the bill you're expected to cover keeps growing. Federal rules already require insurers to publish their negotiated rates — and states are starting to add real enforcement behind them. Ohio's HB 173, for example, gives patients a legal right to see exact hospital prices before they receive care. A right like that only matters if someone makes it usable. That's what VitSee is built to be.

What we believe

Our approach

Transparency by default

If the data exists, you should be able to see it in plain language, not buried in a 2TB JSON file.

Price and quality, together

A low price only matters if the care is good. We rank both together, always.

Built for the real decision

Estimates reflect your actual plan, deductible, coinsurance, and all, not a generic sticker price.

Who we are

We spent decades turning messy data into decisions and actions. We've solved tough problems before; healthcare pricing, buried in terabytes of files nobody actually reads, is just the latest one worth fixing. We came to it with fresh eyes, real technical depth, and no patience for "that's just how healthcare works."
